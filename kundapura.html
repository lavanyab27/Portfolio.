Reading Salt

Making Invisible Coastal Salinity Legible in Kundapura

Water from a household well, an estuary, and the Arabian Sea can look identical in a glass—yet differ by more than 52× in electrical conductivity. Built under the studio Life Imitating Code: Algorithms for Kundapur, this project pairs field conductivity sensing with a real-time TouchDesigner particle network to translate unseen dissolved salt into a responsive field of light.

Honest Measurement Principle: Electrical conductivity counts all dissolved ions together—it cannot separate sodium from beneficial calcium or magnesium, nor detect microbes or nitrates. Every reading on screen is presented as an indicator of salinity, never a medical or lab safety verdict.
02 • Digital Architecture

From Sample to Projection

All logic lives inside a single TouchDesigner network that parses incoming conductivity readings, smooths sensor noise, and drives a real-time POP particle field.

01 SENSE

Probe Reading

EC probe captures sample conductivity (µS/cm) alongside temperature.

02 INPUT

Live Data In

Readings enter TouchDesigner as structured JSON / table data.

03 MAP

Smooth & Scale

Moving average damps flicker; log curve normalises values to 0–1.

04 RESPOND

Particle Field

Particle density, drift velocity, and luminance shift in real time.

05 DISPLAY

Evidence HUD

Sample ID and stabilised µS/cm remain visible beside the particles.

“The Number Never Disappears”

A glowing particle field with no reading beside it would be pure decoration. Only visual motion is smoothed with a short moving average—the displayed µS/cm value stays true to the stabilised sensor reading so the projection remains a translation, not an illustration.

DATs (Data) CHOPs (Channels) SOPs (Geometry) TOPs (Render)
ZONE 01 DATs

Data & JSON Input

execute1, default_data, and in1 ingest live sample readings as JSON text, while json1 and json2 parse keys into structured tables.

ZONE 02 DAT → CHOP

Channel Parsing & Scaling

Parsed tables convert into numeric channels (DAT to CHOP), renamed and rescaled through Math CHOPs for downstream parameter control.

ZONE 03 CHOPs

Motion & Smoothing

Velocity and filter CHOPs compute rate-of-change and damp sensor jitter before merging channels into a clean normalised_data bus.

ZONE 04 DAT / CHOP

State & Pose Matching

Incoming sample and interaction states are reordered and compared against reference thresholds to trigger visual transitions between water types.

ZONE 05 SOPs

Point Geometry Rebuild

Python callbacks rebuild point geometry in real time, attaching custom per-point attributes for spatial distribution, drift, and scale.

ZONE 06 TOPs

Instanced Particle Render

instance_data drives point_render, compositing the luminous particle field and live µS/cm readout out to out1 and the projector.

03 • Visual Language & Iteration

Solving the Logarithmic Mapping Problem

Because field samples span 760 to 39,800 µS/cm, a linear scale erases the exact differences between household wells that matter most to people.

Linear vs. Logarithmic Scale (0–1)

Mapped linearly, all three drinking-water wells collapse near zero—making the screen look equally empty. Switching to a logarithmic scale (500–45,000 µS/cm)—plus a zoomed Drinking-Water Mode (100–3,000 µS/cm)—gives every sample its own legible visual state.

04 • Exhibition Outcome

Dark-Room Water-Testing Installation

Visitors become active testers—dipping the probe into labelled Kundapura samples and watching water that looks identical in the hand transform across the projection wall.

01 Enter

Step into a darkened space dominated by a large projection wall, ambient idle particle motion, and a clear legend explaining EC and the logarithmic scale.

02 Select

Choose from labelled sample stations representing Kundapura's coastal gradient: household wells, river basin, estuary, port, and sea.

03 Measure

Immerse the EC probe into the sample jar to the marked depth and wait a few seconds for the reading to settle.

04 Observe

Watch the live µS/cm reading update on screen as the TouchDesigner particle field expands or contracts in real time.

05 Compare

Rinse the probe in clean water and test a second sample—contrasting two visually identical jars side by side through light and density.

06 Reflect

Wall text grounds the experience: EC makes ionic difference perceptible, while full potability requires laboratory testing.

05 • Reflection

What Worked & Thesis Next Steps

Key Takeaways

  • Tying Art to Evidence: Keeping the live µS/cm reading on screen prevented the TouchDesigner visual from becoming mere aesthetic abstraction.
  • Scale Honesty: Discovering that linear mapping hid well-water variation led directly to the disclosed logarithmic scale and dual exhibition modes.
  • Grounded Fieldwork: Eight standardized samples across five coastal water bodies established a 52× empirical baseline for the digital system.

Next Steps for Thesis

  • Seasonal Resampling: Re-measure the same Kundapura wells pre- and post-monsoon to capture seasonal saltwater intrusion shifts over time.
  • Temperature Compensation: Integrate automated temperature calibration into the live sensor pipeline for multi-hour exhibition runs.
  • Visitor Interaction Studies: Evaluate how first-time visitors interpret the Drinking-Water Mode vs. Coast Mode inside the dark room.
